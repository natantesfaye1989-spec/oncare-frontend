Brand System · v1.1 · October 2026

The steady hand between home and care.

This document is the single source of truth for how OnCare Transit looks, sounds and behaves. The rule behind every rule: say only what a rider, a family or a clinic can check — and make it readable from across the room.

01 · The idea

One promise, three witnesses.

OnCare Transit joins the job — getting people from home to treatment and back — with the feeling of a calm, capable helper on the way. Every rider is picked up in the window we gave, helped from door through door, and brought home again. Families always know what is happening. The rider, the family and the facility can each verify this on any given trip; the brand never claims what they can’t.

What OnCare Transit is not

Not an ambulance

Nothing suggests emergencies: no red, no sirens, no flashing motifs, no language about speed. Our pace is a feature.

Not a taxi or rideshare

We never compete on price or haste. The selling point is dependability and help — a named driver, not a nearby dot on a map.

Not a tech startup

No gradients, no glossy app chrome, no dashboard aesthetics. The brand is people and vans, set in print-like calm.

02 · Voice

Calm, concrete, checkable.

Write the way a good driver talks at the door: unhurried, specific, warm without fuss. Every sentence should survive two tests — read it aloud to someone who is 84, and ask “could they check this?” If it can’t be checked, it isn’t said. No exclamation points. No invented numbers, ever.

03 · Logo

Home, the route, the rider, care.

The mark tells the whole story in one steady line: a home, a calm route, and care at the other end — with the rider as the marigold point we carry between them. It is drawn with the same rounded stroke throughout, so it reads at a glance on a van door or a 16-pixel favicon.

Always

  • Use the two approved colorways: evergreen on light, white on evergreen.
  • Keep the rider dot marigold in both colorways — it is the brand’s one constant.
  • Minimum width 28 px digital / 12 mm print for the mark alone.

Never

  • No red, no gradients, no glows — and nothing that hints at sirens or speed.
  • Don’t rotate, stretch, outline twice, or set the mark over photography without a solid panel.
  • Don’t redraw the heart as a medical cross — care, not clinical.
04 · Color

Evergreen keeps the promise. Marigold carries the person.

A deliberately small palette: deep evergreen for structure and trust, warm marigold for the human moments, on quiet paper. Roughly 60% paper, 30% evergreen, 10% marigold — if a layout feels colorful, it is off-brand. Red appears nowhere; errors use a muted clay, never alarm red.

Contrast, measured

Many riders have low vision. Body text pairs target 7:1 and never fall below 4.5:1; large text and icons never fall below 3:1. These are the only approved text pairs:

05 · Typography

A slab that steadies. A sans built for low vision.

Bitter is the display voice — a slab serif with the matter-of-fact warmth of painted van lettering. Atkinson Hyperlegible carries everything else; it was designed by the Braille Institute specifically for low-vision readers, which makes it a brand decision, not just a font choice. Body text never drops below 19 px, line height 1.6–1.65, line length 70 characters or fewer, sentence case throughout.

06 · Icons & motion

One stroke. One speed.

Icons

A single stroke family: 2 px weight, rounded caps and joins, 24–28 px, drawn on the same grid as the logo. Filled shapes only for the marigold rider dot. Never emoji, never mixed filled/outline sets, never literal medical crosses.

Motion

Motion explains; it never performs. Fades and 16 px rises at 200–260 ms, ease-out in, ease-in out, exits shorter than entrances. Nothing loops, nothing bounces, nothing pulses red. When a visitor prefers reduced motion, everything simply appears — fully readable, nothing lost.
